# AudioSync AirPlay sender specification

A behavioral specification for AudioSync's own AirPlay sender: a Rust crate that finds AirPlay speakers on the local network, opens a session with each one, and plays one shared audio stream on all of them in sync, every speaker with its own delay. It is written so the crate can be built by someone who reads only this document, the RFCs it cites and the documentation of the crates they choose.

AirPlay 1 (also called RAOP or AirTunes v2) is covered in full: enough for a sender that works with open and password-protected speakers, does Apple TV PIN pairing, and keeps several speakers in sync with a per-speaker delay. AirPlay 2 is covered as far as public write-ups go, and section 11 says plainly what they leave out.

Provenance. Written on 2026-10-19 from the public sources listed at the end: community protocol write-ups, project documentation pages, Apple's public documentation and codec description, and the RFCs. Facts are restated in our own words; the community write-ups carry no license, so none of their text is reused. No source code of any AirPlay sender or receiver was consulted, and nothing here describes how AudioSync's current engine works. The Apple TV pairing write-up ends with a short code example; only its prose description and its test vector were used. The community write-ups say they come from reverse engineering and may be incomplete or wrong; where two of them disagree, the clause says so.

## Conventions

- MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are normative, in the sense of RFC 2119.
- Each clause states one behavior of the sender. It ends with *Source:* (where the fact comes from, linked; "derived" means computed from values in a published example rather than stated in words) and, where AudioSync needs something the sources don't settle, *Required:* (the choice the crate MUST implement). "*Source:* AudioSync design" marks a clause that is a requirement only.
- Terms: *sender* is the crate; *receiver* or *speaker* is the AirPlay device; *session* is one RTSP connection plus its UDP flows to one receiver, from connect to teardown; *frame* is one stereo sample pair at 44 100 Hz; *packet* is one RTP audio packet; *host* is the AudioSync app that drives the crate; *sender clock* is defined in P89.
- Multi-byte fields are big-endian (network order) unless a clause says otherwise. Single bytes are written in hex, such as 0xD4. "RTP header without SSRC" means the first 8 bytes of an RFC 3550 header.
- Example addresses use 192.0.2.0/24 (RFC 5737). Numbers quoted from captures come from the cited sources.
- Clause IDs run from P1 without gaps in this version. A clause added later takes the next free number and sits in the section it belongs to; a dropped clause keeps its number, marked withdrawn.

## 1. Scope and role

- **P1** FairPlay and MFi authentication are out of scope. The sender never implements FairPlay (encryption types 3 and 5, `POST /fp-setup`), MFi-SAP (encryption type 4) or the checking of a receiver's MFi certificate, and never uses RSA keys taken from Apple products. A receiver that accepts nothing else is reported as unsupported (P138). *Source:* the unofficial specification states that it gives no RSA keys and leaves FairPlay out ([nto-intro]); AirPlay 2 authentication methods in [ap2i-auth]. *Required:* also out of scope: video, photos, screen mirroring, AAC, AAC-ELD and Opus encoding, multichannel audio, the TCP media transport, and answering remote-control commands (a DACP server).
- **P2** AirPlay 1 is the main path: every speaker that advertises `_raop._tcp` with an encryption mode the sender supports (P45) is driven over AirPlay 1, including AirPlay 2 devices. The AirPlay 2 path (sections 4 and 6) is used only for a speaker that refuses AirPlay 1. *Source:* pyatv lists the HomePod mini among the devices verified with RAOP streaming and says legacy pairing works for RAOP ([pyatv-feat]); shairport-sync can fall back to classic AirPlay ([sps-ap2]). *Required:* the crate MUST be complete for AirPlay 1. The AirPlay 2 path stays behind a build or runtime flag, off by default, until the gaps in section 11 that block it are closed.
- **P3** Each speaker gets its own session: its own RTSP connection, its own UDP sockets (P82), its own RTP sequence numbers, timestamps and SSRC. A failure in one session never stops another session or the audio input. *Source:* AudioSync design; RFC 3550 §8 (one SSRC per source).
- **P4** All speakers of a stream play frames of one shared timeline (P96), each shifted by its own delay (P99). *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P5** The sender names itself AudioSync (`User-Agent: AudioSync/<version>`, the SDP `o=` and `s=` fields). Every identifier it sends (Client-Instance, DACP-ID, Active-Remote, session IDs, AirPlay 2 device ID) is random; an identifier that must persist is a random value created once and stored by the host, never a hardware MAC address, serial number or user name. *Source:* AudioSync design. Captures in [nto-rtsp] and [pyatv-raop] carry other senders' names in these fields; no source says receivers check them (section 11, G11).
- **P6** The call through which the host hands audio to the crate MUST NOT allocate, lock, block, log or touch the file system. It copies frames into a preallocated single-producer, single-consumer ring and returns. All network work runs on the crate's own threads. *Source:* AudioSync design (the app's rule for audio callbacks and the PCM writer path).

## 2. Discovery

- **P7** The sender browses for `_raop._tcp` and `_airplay._tcp` in the `local.` domain over multicast DNS, following PTR records to SRV, TXT and address records. *Source:* RFC 6762; RFC 6763 §4–§6; both service types per [nto-sd] and [oas-sd].
- **P8** A `_raop._tcp` instance name has the form `<id>@<name>`: `<id>` is 12 hex digits (the device's MAC-style identifier without separators) and `<name>` is the speaker name shown to users. *Source:* [nto-raop].
- **P9** A `_airplay._tcp` instance name is the speaker name; its TXT key `deviceid` carries the identifier as six colon-separated hex bytes. *Source:* [nto-ap]; [oas-sd].
- **P10** The sender normalizes both identifiers to 12 uppercase hex digits, and records with the same identifier are one device. The display name is the `_airplay._tcp` instance name when that service exists, otherwise the part of the RAOP name after `@`. The device keeps the SRV target and port of each service it has. *Source:* AudioSync design, from the name forms in [nto-raop] and [nto-ap]. *Required:* a device seen on several interfaces or address families is still one device, and a device with only one of the two services is still listed.
- **P11** Each TXT string is `key=value`. Keys compare case-insensitively; a key without `=` is a boolean true; lists are comma-separated; booleans accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any case; integers accept decimal or `0x` hex. Unknown keys are ignored, and a malformed value drops only that key. *Source:* RFC 6763 §6.4 (keys are case-insensitive); value forms from [nto-raop], [oas-sd] and [pyatv-raop-sd].
- **P12** RAOP TXT keys the sender reads:

  | Key | Meaning | Use |
  |---|---|---|
  | `txtvers` | TXT record version (1) | informational |
  | `ch`, `sr`, `ss` | channels, sample rate, sample size | must be 2, 44100, 16 when present (P16) |
  | `cn` | codecs: 0 PCM, 1 ALAC, 2 AAC, 3 AAC-ELD (pyatv adds 4 Opus) | codec choice (P74) |
  | `et` | encryption types: 0 none, 1 RSA, 3 FairPlay, 4 MFi-SAP, 5 FairPlay SAPv2.5 | pairing mode (P45) |
  | `md` | metadata types: 0 text, 1 artwork, 2 progress | metadata gate (P122) |
  | `pw` | password required | P16 |
  | `da` | digest authentication supported | informational |
  | `tp` | transports, `UDP` and/or `TCP` | must include UDP |
  | `sv`, `sm` | receiver wants the sender to apply volume or mute to the samples | P120 |
  | `sf`, `ft` | status flags and features, hex | P15, P14 |
  | `am`, `vs`, `vn`, `fv`, `ov` | model, server version, protocol version, firmware, OS version | informational, `am` for P46 |
  | `pk` | receiver public key, hex | AirPlay 2 (P66) |

  *Source:* [nto-raop]; [pyatv-raop-sd]; [ap2i-disc].
- **P13** AirPlay TXT keys the sender reads: `deviceid` (P9), `features` (P14), `flags` (P15), `model`, `manufacturer`, `srcvers` (AirPlay version), `protovers`, `pw` (present or true when a password is set), `pk` (32-byte public key in hex), `pi` and `psi` (pairing identities), `gid`, `igl`, `gcgl` (group state), `acl` (access control level), `rsf` (required sender features), `fv`, `osvers`. *Source:* [oas-sd]; [ap2i-disc]; [pyatv-ap-sd].
- **P14** `features` holds one or two hex numbers separated by a comma: the first is bits 0–31, the optional second is bits 32–63. RAOP `ft` is the same bitmask; `/info` reports it as one decimal integer. Bits the sender uses:

  | Bit | Meaning |
  |---|---|
  | 9 | audio supported |
  | 11 | audio redundancy |
  | 14 | FairPlay authentication |
  | 15, 16, 17 | metadata: artwork, progress, text |
  | 18–21 | audio formats; [oas-feat] says 19 and 20 must be set for an AirPlay 2 connection |
  | 23 | RSA authentication |
  | 26 | MFi authentication in [ap2i-feat]; "has unified advertiser info" in [oas-feat] |
  | 27 | legacy pairing supported |
  | 30 | "has unified advertiser info" in [ap2i-feat]; "RAOP on this port" in [oas-feat] |
  | 38, 43, 46, 48 | pairing and encryption (CoreUtils, system, HomeKit, transient) |
  | 40 | buffered audio |
  | 41 | PTP |
  | 50 | binary-plist now-playing metadata |
  | 51 | unified pair-setup with MFi |
  | 52 | extended SETPEERS message |

  *Source:* [oas-sd]; [oas-feat]; [ap2i-feat]; [oas-info]; [pyatv-raop-sd]. *Required:* the two sources disagree on bits 26 and 30, so no decision that can fail a session rests on either bit alone; bit 26 only adds the harmless request of P60.
- **P15** `flags` (AirPlay) and `sf` (RAOP) hold status flags in hex. Bits the sender uses: 2 audio cable attached, 3 PIN required, 7 password required, 9 one-time pairing required, 17 a receiver session is active. *Source:* [oas-flags].
- **P16** For each device the sender computes, and recomputes on every TXT change: *needs password* (RAOP `pw` true, AirPlay `pw` present and not false, or flags bit 7); *needs pairing* (flags bit 3 or 9); *busy hint* (flags bit 17); *AirPlay 1 capable* (has `_raop._tcp`; `et` absent or containing 0, or an Apple TV with legacy pairing (P46); `cn` absent or containing 0 or 1; `tp` absent or containing UDP; `sr`, `ss`, `ch` absent or equal to 44100, 16, 2); *AirPlay 2 capable* (any of features bits 38, 43, 46, 48, with bits 40 and 41 noted). The verdict is reported to the host with the device. *Source:* AudioSync design, from [nto-raop], [oas-flags] and [ap2i-feat]. *Required:* a missing `cn` means ALAC only, the codec of the oldest receivers ([at2]); a missing `et` means "try unencrypted".
- **P17** The sender connects to the addresses of the SRV target in this order: IPv4, IPv6 global, IPv6 link-local (with its interface scope). It never uses an IPv4 link-local address (the 169.254/16 range) while another address exists. The address a session connected to is used for all of its UDP traffic. *Source:* [at2] (the request URI must not use a link-local address); RFC 6762. *Required:* the order.
- **P18** A goodbye record (TTL 0) or an expired record marks the service unavailable for new sessions but never ends a running session; the session's own connection decides that (P140). *Source:* RFC 6762 §10.1; AudioSync design.
- **P19** Receivers change `flags`, `gid`, `igl`, `gcgl` and related keys with their state, for example while another sender plays to them. The sender updates the verdict (P16) and reports it, without touching running sessions. *Source:* [oas-sd] (state changes).

## 3. RTSP session

- **P20** A session is a TCP connection to the `_raop._tcp` SRV port. Requests are RTSP/1.0 text: a request line, header lines, a blank line, and a body sized by `Content-Length`; lines end in CRLF. One request is in flight at a time per connection (no pipelining). *Source:* RFC 2326 §6–§7; [nto-rtsp]. *Required:* the connect times out after 5 s, and a response that doesn't arrive within 10 s is a timeout (P133), except while the sender waits for the user's PIN (P51).
- **P21** Every request carries `CSeq`, starting at 0 on a new connection and going up by one per request. A response MUST carry the CSeq of its request; any other value is a protocol error (P139). *Source:* RFC 2326 §12.17; [ap2i-rtsp] (header table); the start value as in [pyatv-raop].
- **P22** Besides `CSeq`, every request carries `User-Agent: AudioSync/<version>`, `Client-Instance` and `DACP-ID` (the same 16 uppercase hex digits) and `Active-Remote` (a decimal 32-bit number). DACP-ID and Active-Remote announce a remote-control endpoint; AudioSync serves none, so commands a speaker sends there go unanswered. *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [nto-dacp]; [at2] (Client-Instance unique per connection). *Required:* fresh random values for each session (P5).
- **P23** The request URI is `rtsp://<address>/<session-id>`: `<session-id>` is a random 32-bit number in decimal, made once per session, and `<address>` is the sender's own local address on the connection. ANNOUNCE, SETUP, RECORD, SET_PARAMETER, GET_PARAMETER, FLUSH and TEARDOWN use it; OPTIONS uses `*`. *Source:* [pyatv-raop] (the ID matches the `o=` line, and the address is the sender's); [nto-rtsp]; [at2]. Note: AirPlay 2 captures in [ap2i-rtsp] show the receiver's address in the URI, so receivers don't seem to check it. The IPv6 form is unverified (G16).
- **P24** A response starts with `RTSP/1.0 <code> <reason>`; header names are case-insensitive; a missing `Content-Length` means no body. The sender records `Server` and `Audio-Jack-Status` (`connected` or `disconnected`, optionally with `type=analog` or `type=digital`) and reports them; a disconnected jack is a warning, not an error. *Source:* RFC 2326 §7; [nto-rtsp]; [at2] (speaker type from Audio-Jack-Status); responses with `Content-Length: 0` in [pyatv-raop].
- **P25** The `Session` value from the SETUP response goes on every later request of the session (RECORD, SET_PARAMETER, GET_PARAMETER, FLUSH, TEARDOWN). *Source:* [nto-rtsp].
- **P26** AirPlay 1 request order: OPTIONS, then `POST /auth-setup` when it applies (P60), then the legacy pair-verify when the speaker is paired (P55), then ANNOUNCE, SETUP, RECORD, and SET_PARAMETER with the initial volume (P118); audio follows. No audio, sync or retransmission packet is sent before RECORD has been answered with 200. *Source:* [at2] (connect steps); [nto-rtsp]. *Required:* the gating on RECORD.
- **P27** OPTIONS is `OPTIONS * RTSP/1.0`; the response's `Public` header lists the receiver's methods. The sender records the list; a list that lacks ANNOUNCE, SETUP or RECORD marks the receiver unsupported for AirPlay 1, and a missing `Public` header is accepted. The sender sends no `Apple-Challenge` header, which belongs to the RSA mode (P59). *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [pyatv-raop]; [at2] (Apple-Challenge on OPTIONS).
- **P28** ANNOUNCE carries `Content-Type: application/sdp` and an SDP body with these lines, in this order: `v=0`; `o=AudioSync <session-id> 0 IN IP4 <sender address>` (`IP6` for IPv6); `s=AudioSync`; `c=IN IP4 <receiver address>`; `t=0 0`; `m=audio 0 RTP/AVP 96`; `a=rtpmap:96 <encoding>`; and for ALAC `a=fmtp:96 <ALAC configuration>`. No key attributes (`rsaaeskey`, `fpaeskey`, `aesiv`), since the stream is not encrypted. *Source:* RFC 4566 §5; [nto-rtsp]; [pyatv-raop] (`o=` carries the sender's address, `c=` the receiver's); [at2].
- **P29** For ALAC the encoding is `AppleLossless` and the configuration is `352 0 16 40 10 14 2 255 0 0 44100`: frames per packet, compatible version, bit depth, three tuning values (pb, mb, kb), channel count, maximum run, maximum frame size in bytes (0 for unknown), average bit rate (0 for unknown), sample rate. *Source:* [pyatv-raop]; [nto-rtsp]; the field order matches Apple's ALAC configuration record ([alac]).
- **P30** For PCM the encoding is `L16/44100/2` and there is no fmtp line. *Source:* `cn` value 0 means PCM ([nto-raop]); the L16 name and its byte order in RFC 3551 §4.5.11; [at2] sends raw 16-bit big-endian samples. *Required:* PCM is used when `cn` contains 0 (P74). No source shows this exact SDP line from a working sender, so it is checked on hardware before release (G10).
- **P31** SETUP carries `Transport: RTP/AVP/UDP;unicast;interleaved=0-1;mode=record;control_port=<c>;timing_port=<t>`, with the session's local control and timing ports (P82). The response carries `Transport` with `server_port` (audio), `control_port` and `timing_port`, and a `Session` header. `timing_port` may be 0. A response without `server_port` or `control_port` is a protocol error. *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [pyatv-raop] (`timing_port=0` in a response). *Required:* both local sockets are open and answering before SETUP is sent.
- **P32** RECORD carries `Range: npt=0-` and `RTP-Info: seq=<s>;rtptime=<t>`, where s is a random 16-bit value and t a random 32-bit value: the sequence number and RTP timestamp of the first audio packet. The response may carry `Audio-Latency: <frames>` (P110). *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [pyatv-raop] (both values random); [at2].
- **P33** SET_PARAMETER carries the volume (section 8), track information, artwork or progress (section 9), with a `Content-Type` and, for metadata, `RTP-Info: rtptime=<t>`. *Source:* [nto-vol]; [nto-meta].
- **P34** FLUSH carries `RTP-Info: seq=<s>;rtptime=<t>`, naming the first packet that will be sent after the flush; the receiver drops audio queued before it. The sender ignores an `RTP-Info` header in the response. After a flush, numbering continues from s and t, the next sync packet carries the first-packet flag (P93) and the next audio packet the marker (P78). *Source:* [nto-rtsp] (in the published capture, seq and rtptime in FLUSH have advanced by exactly 352 timestamp units per packet since RECORD: derived); [at2] (flags after a flush); [ap2i-rtsp] (FLUSH names the first sequence number and timestamp). Note: [at2] calls the values the last sequence number and timestamp; the derived arithmetic fits the next-packet reading, which the sender uses.
- **P35** TEARDOWN carries `Session` and no body in AirPlay 1. After the response, or after 2 s without one, the sender closes the TCP connection and stops all UDP traffic to that receiver. *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [at2]. *Required:* the 2 s limit.
- **P36** A `401` response with `WWW-Authenticate: Digest realm="<r>", nonce="<n>"` makes the sender send the same request again (with the next CSeq) adding `Authorization: Digest username="iTunes", realm="<r>", nonce="<n>", uri="<request URI>", response="<h>"`. *Source:* [nto-pw] (realm `raop`, user name `iTunes` for audio); RFC 2617 §3.2. *Required:* realm and nonce are copied from the challenge; the user name is the fixed value that section documents for the audio service.
- **P37** Without `qop`, h = MD5(HA1 ":" nonce ":" HA2), with HA1 = MD5(username ":" realm ":" password) and HA2 = MD5(method ":" uri), each MD5 written as 32 lowercase hex digits; `uri` is the request URI exactly as on the request line (`*` for OPTIONS). If the challenge offers `qop=auth`, the sender follows RFC 2617 with `qop`, `nc` and `cnonce`. *Source:* RFC 2617 §3.2.2; [nto-pw] (an exchange without qop).
- **P38** After a challenge, every later request on the same connection carries `Authorization`, recomputed for its own method and URI with the same nonce, until a new 401 brings a new nonce. A 401 to a request that already carried `Authorization` with the current nonce means the password is wrong (P134). *Source:* RFC 2617 §3.3 (reusing a nonce); AudioSync design.
- **P39** The host supplies the password per speaker. When a challenge arrives and the host gave none, the sender stops and reports PasswordRequired (P134). The password never appears in logs or diagnostics. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P40** In AirPlay 1 the receiver sends no requests on the RTSP connection; anything that is not the response to the outstanding request is a protocol error. *Source:* AudioSync design (no source shows receiver-initiated RTSP in AirPlay 1).
- **P41** On the AirPlay 2 path the sender first sends `GET /info RTSP/1.0` with `X-Apple-ProtocolVersion: 1`, `Content-Type: application/x-apple-binary-plist` and the body {`qualifier`: [`txtAirPlay`]}; the answer is a binary plist with the receiver's features, status flags, name, model, protocol and source versions, `pi`, `pk` and similar keys. A second `GET /info` without a body, after the session SETUP, returns further values such as `initialVolume`. AirPlay 2 request bodies are binary plists, and the published AirPlay 2 requests carry no `Session` header. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp]; [oas-info]; [ap2i-proto].
- **P42** AirPlay 2 request order, as published from an Apple sender: `GET /info`; pairing (P63 to P66), with the encrypted channel from then on (P67); session SETUP (P43); `GET /info`; RECORD; SETPEERS (P106); SET_PARAMETER with the volume; stream SETUP (P44); FLUSH with `RTP-Info` naming the first packet; audio; and `POST /feedback` every 2 s throughout (P131). A stream ends with TEARDOWN carrying {`streams`: [{`type`: 96, `streamID`: <id>}]}; the session ends with TEARDOWN carrying an empty dictionary. *Source:* [ap2i-proto]; [ap2i-rtsp]. *Required:* this order on the AirPlay 2 path, the only one documented; where pairing sits in it is an assumption (G4).
- **P43** The session SETUP body holds the sender's details and its timing choice: `deviceID`, `macAddress`, `sessionUUID`, `name`, `model`, `sourceVersion`, `timingProtocol` with its peer information (P104), the group keys `groupUUID`, `groupContainsGroupLeader` and `isMultiSelectAirPlay`, and `et`, `ekey`, `eiv` (seen with `et` 0). The answer holds `eventPort` (P70), `timingPort` and the receiver's `timingPeerInfo`. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp]. *Required:* `name` and `model` are "AudioSync", and `deviceID` and `macAddress` both carry the random persistent identifier (P5); OS fields are left out unless a receiver turns out to need them (G11). What `ekey` and `eiv` do with `et` 0 is not documented.
- **P44** The stream SETUP body is {`streams`: [one dictionary]} with `type` 96 (realtime audio; 103 is buffered audio, 130 remote control), `ct` (compression: 1 LPCM, 2 ALAC, 4 AAC, 8 AAC-ELD, 32 Opus), `audioFormat` (one bit of the format mask, such as 0x40000 for ALAC at 44 100 Hz, 16-bit stereo, or 0x800 for PCM at 44 100 Hz, 16-bit stereo), `spf` (frames per packet), `shk` (P71), the sender's `controlPort`, `latencyMin` and `latencyMax` in frames (11 025 and 88 200 in the capture), `isMedia` true and `audioMode` "default". The answer gives the receiver's `dataPort` and `controlPort`: audio goes to `dataPort` over UDP, control packets to `controlPort`. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp]; [ap2i-audio] (format bits); [ap2i-rt] (realtime audio is RTP over UDP). *Required:* `type` 96, `ct` 2 with `audioFormat` 0x40000, `spf` 352, and the latencies as captured.

## 4. Pairing and encryption

- **P45** Modes: (a) none: `et` contains 0 and neither password nor pairing is needed; (b) password: digest authentication (P36–P39); (c) Apple TV legacy pairing: a one-time PIN pairing plus a verification on every connection, with no encryption of later traffic; (d) RSA (`et` 1 only): unsupported (P59); (e) AirPlay 2 HomeKit-style pairing with an encrypted control channel: partly documented (P61–P71). Password and legacy pairing can apply together. *Source:* [pyatv-feat] (tvOS 10.2 enforced device authentication, "legacy pairing", which only verifies the connection and also works for RAOP, while AirPlay 2 needs HAP-based pairing with encryption); [nto-pw]; [nto-rsa].
- **P46** Legacy pairing applies when the receiver is an Apple TV (`am` or `model` starting with `AppleTV`) and its flags set bit 3 or bit 9, or when an Apple TV without stored credentials refuses ANNOUNCE with a 4xx status other than 401 and 453, which is then reported as PairingRequired rather than Refused (P137). With stored credentials the sender verifies (P55); without them it reports PairingRequired and waits for the host to start pairing. *Source:* [atvpair] (since tvOS 10.2, pairing once and verification on every connection); [oas-flags]; [pyatv-feat]. *Required:* the decision rule.
- **P47** Pairing runs over one TCP connection to the RAOP port (the start request may use its own); requests are `POST <path> RTSP/1.0`; the connection stays open between steps; bodies are binary property lists unless a clause says otherwise. *Source:* [atvpair].
- **P48** `POST /pair-pin-start` with no body; on 200 the Apple TV shows a 4-digit PIN. *Source:* [atvpair]; pyatv notes that `pw` leads a sender to `/pair-pin-start` ([pyatv-raop-sd]).
- **P49** `POST /pair-setup-pin` with `Content-Type: application/x-apple-binary-plist` and the dictionary {`method`: "pin", `user`: client ID}; the response dictionary holds `pk` (the 256-byte SRP value B) and `salt` (16 bytes). *Source:* [atvpair]. *Required:* the client ID is 16 uppercase hex digits of a random 64-bit value, made at pairing and stored with the credentials (P54).
- **P50** SRP-6a over the RFC 5054 2048-bit group (generator 2) with SHA-1, where I is the client ID, P is the PIN as typed (ASCII digits) and s is the salt. The private value a is a fresh random 32-byte secret that also seeds the client's Ed25519 key (a_pub is the Ed25519 public key of a). Two departures from common SRP libraries: the proof is M1 = H(H(N) XOR H(g) | H(I) | s | PAD(A) | PAD(B) | K), with g not padded, and the session key is K = H(S | 00 00 00 00) | H(S | 00 00 00 01), 40 bytes. PAD widens a value to the 256-byte length of N. *Source:* [atvpair]; RFC 5054 §2.5–§2.6 and appendix A; RFC 2945 §3 (shape of M1); RFC 8032 §5.1.5.
- **P51** Between the first and second `/pair-setup-pin` request the sender waits for the host to supply the PIN, with no timeout of its own. If the Apple TV closes the connection meanwhile, pairing ends with PairingCancelled (P135). *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P52** `POST /pair-setup-pin` with {`pk`: A (256 bytes), `proof`: M1 (20 bytes)}; the response holds `proof` (M2). A wrong PIN is answered with status 470. The sender SHOULD check M2 as RFC 2945 defines it, using the K above; the write-up treats the check as optional because the Apple TV rejects a bad exchange itself. *Source:* [atvpair]; RFC 2945 §3.
- **P53** `POST /pair-setup-pin` with {`epk`: 32-byte ciphertext, `authTag`: 16-byte tag}: a_pub sealed with AES-128-GCM and no additional data, where the key is the first 16 bytes of SHA-512("Pair-Setup-AES-Key" | K) and the IV is the first 16 bytes of SHA-512("Pair-Setup-AES-IV" | K) with 1 added to its last byte (modulo 256). A 200 response completes pairing; its body is ignored. The IV is 16 bytes, so the GCM implementation MUST accept a 16-byte nonce. *Source:* [atvpair] (its table gives a larger tag size than its test vector, which has a 16-byte tag; the test vector wins).
- **P54** The credentials are the client ID and the 32-byte secret a. The crate hands them to the host, which stores them securely per speaker; the crate never writes them to disk, and losing them means pairing again. *Source:* [atvpair] (keep the secret); AudioSync design.
- **P55** On every new RTSP connection to a paired Apple TV, before ANNOUNCE (so again after each TEARDOWN): the sender makes a fresh X25519 key pair (v_priv, v_pub) and sends `POST /pair-verify` with `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` and a 68-byte body, 01 00 00 00 | v_pub | a_pub (not a plist). The 96-byte response body is atv_pub (32 bytes) | atv_data (64 bytes). *Source:* [atvpair]; RFC 7748 §5.
- **P56** The sender computes shared = X25519(v_priv, atv_pub) and sig = the Ed25519 signature by a over v_pub | atv_pub, then runs AES-128-CTR with key = first 16 bytes of SHA-512("Pair-Verify-AES-Key" | shared) and IV = first 16 bytes of SHA-512("Pair-Verify-AES-IV" | shared): it first encrypts atv_data and throws that output away, then encrypts sig with the continuing keystream. It sends `POST /pair-verify` with the 68-byte body 00 00 00 00 | encrypted sig; 200 means verified. Traffic after it stays unencrypted. The write-up describes no check of atv_data, and the sender makes none. *Source:* [atvpair]; [pyatv-feat] (no encryption); RFC 8032 §5.1.6.
- **P57** The write-up publishes the intermediate values of one pairing and one verification (its section 10.3). The crate's tests MUST reproduce every one of them. *Source:* [atvpair].
- **P58** A 470 response at any pairing step ends pairing with PairingFailed. A non-200 answer to a verification with stored credentials means they were rejected: the sender reports PairingRequired. *Source:* [atvpair] (status 470); AudioSync design.
- **P59** The RSA mode (`et` 1, early AirPort Express): the sender would put a random 16-byte `Apple-Challenge` on a request, wrap a random AES-128 key with RSA-OAEP under the receiver's RSA public key into SDP `a=rsaaeskey`, send the IV in `a=aesiv` (both base64 without padding), encrypt each packet's audio with AES-128-CBC over whole 16-byte blocks only (a shorter tail stays clear), and check the challenge signed back in `Apple-Response`. What's missing: no allowed source publishes the receiver's RSA public key, so this mode can't be built here. A receiver whose `et` lacks 0 and that isn't an Apple TV with legacy pairing is reported Unsupported. *Source:* [nto-rsa]; [at2] (packetizing encrypted ALAC).
- **P60** A receiver whose RAOP `et` contains 4, or whose features set bit 26 or 51, may refuse further requests until the sender has sent `POST /auth-setup` with `Content-Type: application/octet-stream` and a 33-byte body: 0x01 (unencrypted) followed by a fresh X25519 public key. The response (the receiver's key, an MFi certificate and a signature) is the receiver proving itself; the sender reads it and discards it without checking (P1). *Source:* [oas-auth]; [pyatv-authsetup] (send it and ignore the answer); [ap2i-auth]. *Required:* sent once per connection, right after OPTIONS, for such receivers; a failed `/auth-setup` is not fatal and the session goes on.
- **P61** AirPlay 2 pairing is transient when the receiver supports it (features bit 48 or 43) and needs no PIN (flags bit 3 clear), and PIN-based otherwise. Who may play to a speaker is set in Apple's Home app: everyone, anyone on the same network, or only people sharing the home, optionally with a password. Which of these settings allow a transient pairing, and how the password enters a PIN pairing, is not documented (G4). *Source:* [ap2i-feat] (transient pairing is implied by bit 48 or 43); [oas-hkp]; [apple-home] (the access settings).
- **P62** AirPlay 2 pairing data is TLV8: each item is a type byte, a length byte and the value. A value longer than 255 bytes is split into consecutive items of the same type, all but the last 255 bytes long, and received items are joined the same way. Type numbers seen: 0 method, 1 identifier, 2 salt, 3 public key, 4 proof, 5 encrypted data, 6 state, 10 signature; type 27 appears in a pair-setup reply with no known meaning. *Source:* derived from the decoded pair-setup and pair-verify messages in [pyatv-pair] and [pyatv-verify] (a 384-byte public key arrives as items of 255 and 129 bytes); pyatv says the pairing payload is HAP TLV8.
- **P63** Pair-setup is `POST /pair-setup` with a TLV8 body. M1 (sender) {method 0, state 1}; M2 (receiver) {state 2, salt (16 bytes), public key (384 bytes)}; M3 (sender) {state 3, public key A (384 bytes), proof (64 bytes)}; M4 (receiver) {state 4, proof (64 bytes)}. The sizes point to SRP-6a over the RFC 5054 3072-bit group with SHA-512. Not documented: the SRP user name, how M1 marks a transient pairing, and the request's content type (G4). *Source:* message shapes and sizes derived from [pyatv-pair] (the Companion protocol wraps the same HAP exchange in its own frames); the endpoint per [oas-hkp].
- **P64** A transient pairing ends after M4: both sides use the SRP shared secret as the session's encryption key, and no pair-verify follows. The default code for this mode is 3939. *Source:* [oas-hkp].
- **P65** A PIN pairing continues with M5 (sender: encrypted data holding its identifier, its Ed25519 long-term public key and a signature) and M6 (receiver: the same for itself), and then a pair-verify on every connection. The key for such encrypted items comes from HKDF-SHA-512 over the SRP shared secret with salt "Pair-Setup-Encrypt-Salt" and info "Pair-Setup-Encrypt-Info", as documented for the MFi variant of M4 (nonce "PS-Msg04"); the nonces of M5 and M6 and the exact signed data are not documented (G4). *Source:* [pyatv-pair]; [oas-hkp] (MFi authentication). *Required:* the crate implements the documented pieces (TLV8, SRP with a configurable user name, the framing below) behind the AirPlay 2 flag (P2) and completes the exchange only once G4 is closed.
- **P66** Pair-verify is `POST /pair-verify` with TLV8 bodies: M1 {state 1, X25519 public key (32 bytes)}; M2 {state 2, public key (32 bytes), encrypted data (120 bytes: 104 bytes of TLV8 holding an identifier of 36 bytes and a 64-byte Ed25519 signature, plus a 16-byte tag)}; M3 {state 3, encrypted data (120 bytes)}; M4 {state 4}. The session key is the one the two sides agree here. Its derivation, the nonces and the signed data are not documented (G4). *Source:* derived from [pyatv-verify]; [oas-hkp].
- **P67** Once a transient pair-setup or a pair-verify has completed, every byte on that RTSP connection, in both directions, travels in frames: a 2-byte little-endian length N, N bytes of ChaCha20-Poly1305 ciphertext, then the 16-byte tag. An RTSP message may span several frames. *Source:* [oas-hkp]; [pyatv-enc2]. *Required:* the sender writes frames of at most 1024 plaintext bytes (the HomeKit limit, which pyatv says AirPlay receivers don't insist on) and accepts any N up to 65 535.
- **P68** The sender-to-receiver key is HKDF-SHA-512 with the session's encryption key as input, salt "Control-Salt", info "Control-Write-Encryption-Key" and 32 bytes of output; the receiver-to-sender key uses info "Control-Read-Encryption-Key". *Source:* [oas-hkp]; RFC 5869.
- **P69** Each direction keeps a 64-bit frame counter that starts at 0 and goes up by one per frame; the 12-byte ChaCha20-Poly1305 nonce is four zero bytes followed by the counter in little-endian; the additional data is the frame's 2-byte length field. *Source:* the counter and its zero high bits per [oas-hkp]; the little-endian counter and "frame header as additional data" per pyatv's Companion encryption ([pyatv-enc]); RFC 8439 §2.8. *Required:* this layout is inferred and MUST be confirmed against a real receiver before release (G6).
- **P70** The first AirPlay 2 SETUP response names an `eventPort`. The sender opens a TCP connection to it and frames it like P67, with keys from salt "Events-Salt" and infos "Events-Write-Encryption-Key" and "Events-Read-Encryption-Key", roles swapped: the sender encrypts with the Read key and decrypts with the Write key. The receiver sends RTSP requests on it (such as `POST /command` with a binary plist); the sender MUST answer each with `RTSP/1.0 200 OK` and the same CSeq, or the receiver closes the connection after about 30 s. *Source:* [pyatv-ap2] (event channel); [ap2i-rtsp] (the event channel must be open before the session goes on).
- **P71** In AirPlay 2 realtime audio, the stream SETUP carries `shk`, a random 32-byte key. Each RTP audio payload is sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 under it, with the additional data being RTP header bytes 4–11 (timestamp and SSRC), and the packet ends with a 24-byte trailer: an 8-byte nonce, then the 16-byte tag. *Source:* [ap2i-rtp]. *Required:* the nonce is a 64-bit counter per session, never repeated under one key; the trailer order and how the 8-byte nonce fills the 12-byte one come from one source and are confirmed on hardware (G7).

## 5. Audio

- **P72** The stream is 44 100 Hz, 16-bit signed, 2 channels, left then right. *Source:* RAOP TXT `sr`, `ss`, `ch` ([nto-raop]); classic AirPlay offers only 16-bit 44.1 kHz stereo ([sps-ap2]); [at2].
- **P73** The host hands the crate interleaved stereo frames at 44 100 Hz, as float32 or signed 16-bit, already resampled. The crate converts float to 16-bit by multiplying by 32 767, rounding to nearest and clamping to the 16-bit range; a NaN or infinite sample becomes 0. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P74** Codec per speaker: PCM when `cn` contains 0; otherwise ALAC when `cn` contains 1 or is absent; otherwise the speaker is unsupported. If an ANNOUNCE offering PCM is refused with a 4xx status other than 401 and 453 and `cn` also contains 1, the sender retries once with ALAC on a new connection and remembers the choice for that device. *Source:* `cn` values in [nto-raop]; AudioSync design (G10).
- **P75** Every packet holds 352 frames (about 7.98 ms) with either codec. *Source:* [at2] (frames per packet); the first fmtp value in [nto-rtsp].
- **P76** A PCM payload is 352 frames × 2 channels × 2 bytes = 1408 bytes, each sample big-endian, left before right. With the 12-byte header the datagram fits a 1500-byte MTU over IPv4 and IPv6. *Source:* RFC 3551 §4.5.11 (L16 is in network byte order); [at2] (raw L16 converted to big-endian).
- **P77** An ALAC payload is one ALAC frame of exactly 352 frames, produced by an ALAC encoder set up as P29 (16-bit, 2 channels, 44 100 Hz, frame length 352); the encoder may emit compressed or uncompressed frames. The ALAC bitstream is defined by Apple's open-source codec, not here. *Source:* [alac]; [nto-rtsp].
- **P78** The RTP header is 12 bytes: byte 0 is 0x80 (version 2, no padding, no extension, no CSRC); byte 1 is 0x60 (payload type 96), or 0xE0 (marker set) on the first packet after RECORD or FLUSH; bytes 2–3 the sequence number; bytes 4–7 the RTP timestamp; bytes 8–11 the SSRC, random per session and fixed within it. The payload follows. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (marker rule and a captured header starting 80 E0); [at2]; RFC 3550 §5.1.
- **P79** The first packet uses the sequence number and timestamp announced in RECORD; each later packet adds 1 to the sequence number (modulo 2^16) and 352 to the timestamp (modulo 2^32). Comparisons are modular. *Source:* [at2]; RFC 3550 §5.1.
- **P80** The shared timeline (P96) is cut into packets at frame indices that are multiples of 352. Every speaker gets the same packet contents, encoded once per packet, each with its own header; a speaker that joins late starts at a packet boundary. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P81** For speaker s, frame n has RTP timestamp (k_s + n) modulo 2^32, so packet j (frames 352·j to 352·j + 351) carries (k_s + 352·j) modulo 2^32. k_s is chosen at RECORD so the first packet gets the announced rtptime, and chosen again at each FLUSH so numbering continues from the value the FLUSH names (P34). *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P82** Each session opens two local UDP sockets on ephemeral ports, in the address family of its RTSP connection: control (announced as `control_port`) and timing (announced as `timing_port`). *Source:* AudioSync design; [at2] lists customary ports (6001, 6002) that the sender doesn't need.
- **P83** Audio packets go to the receiver's `server_port`; sync packets and retransmission replies go to its `control_port`; all three are sent from the session's control socket. *Source:* [nto-rtsp] (channel roles); [nto-rtp] (ports per payload type). *Required:* the sending socket.
- **P84** Packet j is due to leave for speaker s at c(352·j + 351) + d_s: when its last frame has arrived from the host (P96), plus the speaker's delay (P99). The sender sends each packet within 5 ms of that time and never more than 20 ms early; a packet already past its time is sent at once (P112). *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P85** While a session plays, packets never pause; when the host has no audio, they carry digital silence. *Source:* AudioSync design. (One write-up notes that iTunes tears a session down after 2 s of suspended audio ([at2]); a continuous stream avoids that path.)
- **P86** Retransmission requests reach the session's control socket from the receiver: 8 bytes, 0x80 0xD5, a 2-byte sequence field, then the first missing sequence number (2 bytes) and the number of missing packets (2 bytes). *Source:* [nto-rtp] (retransmit packets); [at2] (resend packet).
- **P87** For each requested packet still held, the sender sends to the receiver's control port 0x80 0xD6, a 2-byte sequence field holding that packet's sequence number, then the complete RTP packet exactly as first sent (header and payload). Packets no longer held are skipped without a reply. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (the reply carries a full audio RTP packet after the sequence field); [ap2i-rtcp] (reply type 214, which is 0xD6). *Required:* the value of the sequence field.
- **P88** Each session can resend its last 1024 packets (about 8.2 s); since payloads are shared (P80), one payload store serves all sessions, and each session keeps only its headers. A request for more than 128 packets is clipped to the first 128, and a packet is resent at most 3 times. *Source:* [at2] (a backlog of 1000 packets); AudioSync design.

## 6. Timing

- **P89** The sender clock is one monotonic clock for all sessions, never stepped or slewed by wall-clock changes, read as a 64-bit NTP-format timestamp (32-bit seconds, 32-bit fraction). By convention its seconds count from 0x83AA7E80 (the Unix epoch in NTP seconds) plus the time since the clock's origin. *Source:* [at2] (sender and devices use a monotonic clock in NTP format with that origin); RFC 5905 §6. *Required:* the host schedules its local outputs on the same clock, so the crate either exposes its clock reads or takes the host's clock.
- **P90** Once a session is set up, the receiver sends 32-byte timing requests to the sender's timing port, about every 3 s: 0x80 0xD2, a 2-byte sequence number, four zero bytes, then three NTP timestamps (origin, receive, transmit), of which only transmit (the receiver's send time) is filled in. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (timing packets and a captured request); [at2].
- **P91** The sender answers each request with 32 bytes to the request's source address and port: 0x80 0xD3, the request's sequence number, four zero bytes, origin = the request's transmit timestamp copied unchanged, receive = sender clock when the request arrived, transmit = sender clock just before sending. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (a captured reply); [at2]. *Required:* both clock reads sit as close to the socket calls as possible, on a thread that does nothing slow, so processing stays under 1 ms; the reply goes to the request's source even when SETUP returned `timing_port` 0.
- **P92** In AirPlay 1 the sender sends no timing requests of its own and ignores anything on the timing socket that isn't a request. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (the receiver asks, the sender answers); AudioSync design.
- **P93** A sync packet is 20 bytes to the receiver's control port: byte 0 is 0x90 on the first sync after RECORD or FLUSH (extension bit set) and 0x80 otherwise; byte 1 is 0xD4; bytes 2–3 a sequence field; bytes 4–7 the RTP timestamp of the frame due to be heard at the time in bytes 8–15; bytes 8–15 a sender-clock time in NTP format; bytes 16–19 the RTP timestamp of the next audio packet the sender will send to this receiver. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (sync packets); [at2] (sync packet fields: now minus latency, time, now). *Required:* the sequence field counts up from 0 per session; captures show a counter-like value in one source and a constant 7 in another, so receivers aren't expected to read it.
- **P94** One sync goes right before the first audio packet after RECORD and after each FLUSH, then one per 44 100 frames of stream (once a second), each just before the audio packet whose timestamp it carries in bytes 16–19. *Source:* [nto-rtp]; [at2].
- **P95** The receiver lines up its playback so that the frame named in bytes 4–7 is heard at the sender-clock time in bytes 8–15, using the timing exchange to translate sender time into its own; bytes 16–19 minus bytes 4–7 is the latency the sender asks for, in frames. In the published capture that difference is 77 175 frames (1.75 s). *Source:* field names in [at2]; [nto-rtp] (sync packets tie RTP timestamps to the NTP clock); the number derived from the sync example in [nto-rtp]; shairport-sync says the source sets the latency ([sps-readme]). *Required:* this reading is the model the sender follows. How exactly each receiver applies it (for example whether it adds its own `Audio-Latency`) is checked on hardware (G9).
- **P96** The frames from the host, plus any silence the crate inserts (P112), are numbered n = 0, 1, 2, … from the start of the stream. c(n) is the sender-clock time at which frame n arrived from the host, as given by the rate model (P101). Speaker s with delay d_s hears frame n at P_s(n) = c(n) + L + d_s, where L = Lf / 44 100 s is the base latency (P98). *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P97** For speaker s, with j the next packet to send: bytes 16–19 = the RTP timestamp of frame 352·j; bytes 4–7 = the RTP timestamp of frame 352·j − Lf (P81, modulo 2^32); bytes 8–15 = P_s(352·j − Lf) as the model gives it, not the moment the packet happens to leave. Because packet j leaves at about c(352·j + 351) + d_s, the stated time lies within about 10 ms of the send time. *Source:* AudioSync design, applying P95.
- **P98** The base latency Lf defaults to 77 175 frames (1.75 s), the value in the published capture. The host may set any value from 11 025 to 88 200 frames (0.25 to 2.0 s), the range an Apple sender announced in an AirPlay 2 SETUP. All speakers of a stream use the same Lf; changing it flushes every session, as a capture restart does (P142). *Source:* derived from [nto-rtp]; `latencyMin` and `latencyMax` in [ap2i-rtsp]; classic sources run at about 2.0 to 2.25 s ([sps-readme]). *Required:* the default and the range.
- **P99** Each speaker has a delay d_s from 0 to 3000 ms, set by the host. The sender applies it in two places at once: it sends that speaker's packets d_s later (P84) and states times d_s later in its sync packets (P96). The latency field (bytes 16–19 minus bytes 4–7) stays Lf for every speaker, so a receiver's buffer fill doesn't change with its delay. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P100** A delay change for a playing speaker takes effect through a FLUSH of that speaker: the sender stops sending to it, sends FLUSH, and resumes with the next packet due under the new delay. That speaker is silent for about L; the others are untouched. Changes arriving within 250 ms of each other are merged into one flush. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P101** c(n) is piecewise linear: c(n) = c(n_u) + (n − n_u) / R̂ from the last update at frame n_u. R̂ starts at 44 100 Hz and follows the host's actual frame rate measured on the sender clock, as a least-squares fit of (frames delivered, arrival time) over the last 30 s or an equivalent delay-locked loop. R̂ changes only at sync points, stays within ±1000 ppm of 44 100 Hz, and each change moves the predicted time of any future frame by at most 0.5 ms. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P102** All speakers share one c(n): for every frame n and any two speakers s1 and s2, P_s1(n) − P_s2(n) = d_s1 − d_s2 at all times, within one frame. This is the multi-room guarantee. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P103** A speaker that joins a playing stream starts, after its RECORD, with the next packet in its regular schedule (P84), preceded by a first-flagged sync; it is heard about L later, in step with the others. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P104** In AirPlay 2 the first SETUP names `timingProtocol`: "PTP", with `timingPeerInfo` (`Addresses`, `ID`, `SupportsClockPortMatchingOverride`) and `timingPeerList`, or "NTP". Under PTP the receiver answers with `timingPort` 0 and its own `timingPeerInfo`; under NTP it opens a timing port and the AirPlay 1 exchange applies. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp].
- **P105** PTP in AirPlay 2 runs over UDP ports 319 and 320 and uses part of IEEE 1588-2008; one receiver's `/info` describes its PTP stack with a string that mentions OpenAVNU and aPTP. Which messages are used, which side is the grandmaster, the domain, the intervals and how RTP time maps onto PTP time are not documented (G1). *Source:* [sps-ap2] (ports 319 and 320); [nqptp] (part of IEEE 1588-2008); [oas-info] (the `PTPInfo` example).
- **P106** SETPEERS has `Content-Type: /peer-list-changed` and a binary-plist array of address strings (every group member's addresses, the receivers' and the sender's); it is sent after RECORD and whenever the group changes. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp]; [ap2i-proto]; [pyatv-raop] (SETPEERS).
- **P107** Under PTP the sender sends "time announce" control packets (second byte 0xD7) instead of 0xD4 sync packets; they carry an RTP time, an 8-byte network time and fields whose meaning is not documented (G1). *Source:* [ap2i-rtcp]. Note: that page gives the timing request and reply as 211 and 212, one more than the 0xD2 and 0xD3 that [nto-rtp], [oas-rtp] and [at2] give; the sender uses 0xD2 and 0xD3.
- **P108** When the AirPlay 2 path is used, the sender asks for "NTP" timing and applies this section unchanged; if a receiver won't take NTP timing, AirPlay 2 audio to it is unsupported until G1 is closed. *Source:* AudioSync design; the NTP option per [ap2i-rtsp] and the `forceAirPlay2NTP` setting named in [ap2i-feat].

## 7. Buffering and latency

- **P109** Receivers buffer about the latency the sender asks for. Classic AirPlay sources set about 2.0 to 2.25 s and AirPlay 2 can go down to about half a second; AirPlay 2 realtime audio plays about two seconds late, while buffered audio starts sooner and may arrive faster than real time. *Source:* [sps-readme]; [sps-ap2].
- **P110** Receivers report their own output latency: in AirPlay 1 as the `Audio-Latency` header of the RECORD response, in frames (captures show 2205 and 3035); in AirPlay 2 as `audioLatencies` entries with `outputLatencyMicros` in `/info` (400 000 in one capture). One older write-up treats `Audio-Latency` from some receivers as unreliable. *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [pyatv-raop]; [ap2i-rtsp]; [at2]. *Required:* the sender records and reports these values and never folds them into Lf or the timeline; per-speaker compensation stays with the host, through d_s.
- **P111** Per stream the sender keeps an input ring of at least 1 s (host to crate) and one payload store that covers the resend history (P88) plus the largest delay (3 s). At 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo, 12 s of audio is about 2.1 MB. All buffers are allocated when the stream starts. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P112** When a packet's send time comes before the host has delivered all its frames, the sender fills the missing frames with silence, sends on time and counts the silent frames; frames for that range that arrive later are dropped, so the timeline never stalls or shifts. When the input ring is full, the host's write keeps what fits, and the dropped frames are counted. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P113** For each speaker the crate reports the delay from a frame's arrival to its sound, L + d_s, and separately the receiver's reported latency; on request it gives c(n) for any n, so the host can line local outputs up with the AirPlay speakers. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P114** A speaker's first packet is heard about L after it is sent. The crate reports, per speaker, the sender-clock time at which that first packet is due to be heard. *Source:* AudioSync design.

## 8. Volume

- **P115** Volume is a SET_PARAMETER with `Content-Type: text/parameters` and the body `volume: <dB>` followed by CRLF. −144 means muted; otherwise the value runs from −30 to 0. *Source:* [nto-vol]; [at2].
- **P116** The number is written in fixed point with six decimals ("-11.123877", "-20.000000"), with a period as the decimal separator whatever the system locale, and no exponent. *Source:* captures in [nto-vol] and [ap2i-rtsp]. *Required:* six decimals.
- **P117** The host sets a speaker's volume as a dB value from −30 to 0, or mute; values outside the range are clamped and NaN is ignored. How the app's slider maps to dB is the host's choice. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P118** The initial volume goes out right after RECORD succeeds, before or together with the first audio. AirPlay 2 receivers report their current level as `initialVolume` (−144 to 0) in `/info`. *Source:* [at2] (connect steps); [ap2i-rtsp].
- **P119** At most one volume request is in flight per session; a newer value replaces one still queued; at most 10 volume requests per second go to one session; volume requests go ahead of queued metadata. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P120** A receiver whose RAOP TXT has `sv=true` wants the sender to apply the volume to the samples: the sender multiplies that speaker's samples by 10^(dB/20) (0 when muted) before encoding and sends it no volume requests. `sm=true` asks the same for mute alone. *Source:* [pyatv-raop-sd] (`sv`, `sm`). *Required:* such a speaker gets its own encoding, outside the shared payloads (P80); this rests on a single source.
- **P121** The sender MAY read a receiver's volume with GET_PARAMETER (`Content-Type: text/parameters`, body `volume`, answer body `volume: <dB>`) to show it to the host; the answer never changes the sender's own state. *Source:* [ap2i-rtsp].

## 9. Metadata and artwork

- **P122** The sender sends only the metadata types a receiver lists: RAOP `md` 0 (text), 1 (artwork), 2 (progress); on the AirPlay 2 path, features bits 17, 15 and 16. *Source:* [nto-raop]; [oas-sd]; [ap2i-feat].
- **P123** Track information is a SET_PARAMETER with `Content-Type: application/x-dmap-tagged` and `RTP-Info: rtptime=<t>`, where t is the RTP timestamp from which it applies. The body is DMAP: each chunk is a 4-character code, a 4-byte big-endian length of its data, then the data. It holds an `mlit` container with `minm` (title), `asar` (artist) and `asal` (album) as UTF-8 strings. *Source:* [nto-meta] (the three fields shown); [pyatv-proto] (DMAP binary format); [daap] (the codes for dmap.listingitem, dmap.itemname, daap.songartist and daap.songalbum). *Required:* the `mlit` container, which the sources don't show for this request.
- **P124** Artwork is a SET_PARAMETER with `Content-Type: image/jpeg`, `RTP-Info: rtptime=<t>` and a JPEG body. *Source:* [nto-meta]. *Required:* JPEG only, at most 512 KiB; the host scales or drops larger images.
- **P125** Progress is a SET_PARAMETER with `Content-Type: text/parameters` and the body `progress: <start>/<current>/<end>`, three absolute RTP timestamps. *Source:* [nto-meta]. *Required:* AudioSync plays live audio without track bounds, so it sends progress only when the host supplies a track's start and length.
- **P126** Metadata is optional and never delays audio or volume: the host supplies now-playing information when it has some; metadata requests wait while a volume request is pending; a failed metadata request is a warning and never ends the session. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P127** Binary-plist now-playing metadata (features bit 50) is not documented and not sent. *Source:* [ap2i-feat]; [oas-sd].

## 10. Teardown, keep-alive and errors

- **P128** Stopping a speaker: stop its packets, send TEARDOWN (P35), wait at most 2 s for the answer, close the connection. Stopping the stream does this for every speaker in parallel and finishes within 2 s overall. *Source:* [nto-rtsp]; [at2] (disconnect); AudioSync design for the limits.
- **P129** The crate never ends a session on its own because the audio is silent; the host decides when a speaker stops. *Source:* AudioSync design (compare [at2], where iTunes tears down after 2 s of suspended audio).
- **P130** In AirPlay 1 the continuous audio and the receiver's timing requests keep a session alive; no keep-alive request is documented. The sender SHOULD send `OPTIONS *` after 30 s without any RTSP request on the connection, to notice a dead connection early. A session that has received at least one timing request and then receives none for 10 s is lost (P140); a session that never received one is judged by its connection alone. *Source:* [nto-rtp] (requests every 3 s); [pyatv-raop] (a receiver that returns `timing_port` 0); AudioSync design.
- **P131** On the AirPlay 2 path the sender sends `POST /feedback` on the RTSP connection every 2 s for as long as the session lasts; the answer is 200 with a binary plist. Three missing or failed answers in a row mean the speaker is lost. *Source:* [pyatv-ap2] (iOS sends it every two seconds as a keep-alive); [ap2i-rtsp] (sent until disconnect). *Required:* the loss rule.
- **P132** Errors are typed events per speaker. None of them panics, ends another session or stops the input. Each carries the RTSP method and status code when there is one. Kinds: Unreachable, Timeout, PasswordRequired, WrongPassword, PairingRequired, PairingFailed, PairingCancelled, Busy, Refused, Unsupported (with the reason: encryption, codec, transport, methods or format), ProtocolError, Lost. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P133** A TCP connect that is refused, unreachable or unanswered for 5 s gives Unreachable; a response missing after 10 s gives Timeout and closes the session. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P134** A 401 when the host gave no password gives PasswordRequired; a 401 to a request that carried `Authorization` with the current nonce gives WrongPassword. Neither is retried automatically. *Source:* [nto-pw]; RFC 2617; AudioSync design.
- **P135** A 470 during pairing gives PairingFailed; a failed verification gives PairingRequired (the stored credentials are no longer accepted); the receiver closing the connection while the sender waits for a PIN gives PairingCancelled. *Source:* [atvpair] (status 470); AudioSync design.
- **P136** Flags bit 17 seen before connecting is reported as a busy hint, and the sender still tries. A 453 answer to ANNOUNCE, SETUP or RECORD gives Busy. No source documents which status a receiver uses while another sender holds it; 453 ("Not Enough Bandwidth" in RFC 2326) is an assumption to confirm on hardware (G8). *Source:* [oas-flags]; RFC 2326 §11.3; AudioSync design.
- **P137** Any other 4xx or 5xx answer to OPTIONS, ANNOUNCE, SETUP or RECORD gives Refused, with method and code, and closes the session. A 4xx or 5xx answer to SET_PARAMETER, GET_PARAMETER or FLUSH is a warning only. *Source:* RFC 2326 §7.1; AudioSync design.
- **P138** Unsupported is reported from discovery or on connect when there is no usable encryption mode (P59), no usable codec (P74), no UDP transport, an OPTIONS list without the needed methods (P27), or a format other than 44 100 Hz, 16-bit stereo. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P139** A malformed status line, a wrong CSeq, a missing required header or an unexpected request from the receiver gives ProtocolError and closes the session. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P140** A speaker is lost when its TCP connection closes or resets, when two UDP sends to it in a row fail with an unreachable error, or when the rule in P130 or P131 fires. The session's resources are released and the other speakers keep playing. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P141** While the host still wants a lost speaker, the crate retries after 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 s and then every 30 s, each attempt a complete new session (new identifiers, sequence numbers and timestamps). On success the speaker joins the running timeline (P103) and plays in step with the others. Retries stop on PasswordRequired, WrongPassword, PairingRequired, PairingFailed and Unsupported, which need the user. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P142** When the host restarts its capture (a new source or a rate change), it tells the crate, which flushes every session, resets the rate model and the frame count, and resumes; the speakers fall silent for about L and come back in step. *Source:* AudioSync design.
- **P143** Counters the host can read without locks: per session, packets sent, retransmission requests received, packets resent, requested packets no longer held, timing requests answered, late packets, silent frames inserted and input frames dropped; per stream, the current R̂. *Source:* AudioSync design.

## 11. Gaps

What a sender needs that no public source covered when this was written. Each gap names the clauses it blocks and what would close it.

- **G1 PTP group timing.** AirPlay 2 multi-room timing runs on PTP (P105, P107). Undocumented: the message set Apple uses, which device is the grandmaster, the domain and intervals, how the sender's clock identity reaches receivers, and how RTP timestamps map onto PTP time in the time-announce packet. Closing it needs a public write-up, or black-box captures of an Apple sender and receivers on one's own network, which is the clean-room way to learn it. Until then the AirPlay 2 path asks for NTP timing (P108).
- **G2 SETPEERS.** Only the address list is documented (P106). Unknown: what the extended message (features bit 52) adds, whether the sender's addresses must come first or last, and what a receiver does when the list changes mid-stream.
- **G3 Buffered-audio SETUP.** Stream type 103 (TCP, each RTP packet preceded by a 16-bit length) and `FLUSHBUFFERED` with `flushFromSeq`, `flushFromTS`, `flushUntilSeq` and `flushUntilTS` are named in [ap2i-buf] and [oas-flushb], but the SETUP fields for buffered audio, the AAC format it usually carries, and how playback start is anchored in time are not documented. AudioSync doesn't need buffered audio for live capture; it is listed for completeness.
- **G4 HomeKit pairing details.** For AirPlay 2 pair-setup and pair-verify, public sources give the message shapes, sizes, the control-channel key labels and the transient code, but not: the SRP user name; how M1 marks a transient pairing; the request content type; the HKDF labels and nonces for M5, M6 and pair-verify; what exactly is signed in M5, M6, M2 and M3 of verify; the error TLV values; and how a Home-app speaker password enters the exchange. These live in Apple's HomeKit Accessory Protocol specification, whose non-commercial edition needs an Apple ID and acceptance of Apple's license terms. Whether those terms suit a distributed open-source app is a licensing question for the maintainer, not something the crate author can settle; until it is settled, P63 to P66 stay incomplete.
- **G5 Apple TV Companion pairing.** pyatv documents the Companion protocol's HomeKit-style pairing and OPACK frames for remote control ([pyatv-pair]). Nothing public says whether an audio sender needs it to stream to newer Apple TVs over AirPlay 2, or how it relates to AirPlay 2 pairing with an on-screen PIN. AirPlay 1 with legacy pairing (P46) is the documented path to Apple TVs.
- **G6 Encrypted framing details.** The nonce layout and the additional data of the control channel (P69) are inferred from the Companion protocol; one session against a real receiver confirms or corrects them.
- **G7 AirPlay 2 audio packet trailer.** The order of nonce and tag and how the 8-byte nonce fills the 12-byte ChaCha20 nonce (P71) rest on a single diagram in one source.
- **G8 Busy and access statuses.** No source documents the status code for "another sender is playing" (P136) or for a speaker restricted to people sharing a home. Hardware tests settle the mapping.
- **G9 Receivers' reading of sync packets.** That receivers play the frame in bytes 4–7 at the time in bytes 8–15 (P95) follows from field names and descriptions, not from a statement; whether they add their own `Audio-Latency` on top is not documented. A two-speaker ear test with a known delay settles it, and the host's per-speaker delay covers any constant offset meanwhile.
- **G10 PCM over AirPlay 1.** The exact SDP for PCM (P30) isn't shown by any capture, and one write-up sets the packet's payload type to 10 (the static L16 stereo type of RFC 3551) for raw PCM instead of 96. The sender uses 96 with `L16/44100/2` and falls back to ALAC if a receiver refuses PCM; hardware tests decide whether the fallback is ever needed.
- **G11 Sender names.** No source says whether receivers check `User-Agent` or the SDP `o=` and `s=` names (P5). One sender's changelog records switching to an AirPlay-style user agent for Apple TVs and HomePods ([libraop-changelog]), which suggests some receivers look at it. If hardware tests confirm that, using such a token is a decision for the maintainer, since it would mean presenting as another sender.
- **G12 Features bits 26 and 30.** The two community sources give different meanings (P14); no decision rests on them alone.
- **G13 Binary-plist metadata.** Features bit 50 metadata (P127) has no public format.
- **G14 TCP-only receivers.** A receiver whose `tp` lists only TCP needs RTP over TCP, whose framing for AirPlay 1 isn't documented in these sources; such receivers are unsupported.
- **G15 RSA receivers.** The RSA mode (P59) needs a receiver public key that no allowed source publishes; such receivers are unsupported by design.
- **G16 IPv6 request URIs.** The one IPv6 capture writes the address without brackets in the URI (P23); RFC 3986 would bracket it. The sender prefers IPv4 (P17); the IPv6 form is checked on hardware.
