Free and open source · for macOS 15, Windows 11 and Linux

Every speaker in the house, on the same beat.

AudioSync takes your computer's audio, on a Mac, a Windows PC or Linux, and plays it on AirPlay speakers, Google Cast speakers and the computer's own outputs, Bluetooth included, all at once. It lines them up, so you hear one song instead of an echo. Think of it as a free Airfoil alternative that does less, on purpose.

One computer, every speaker

I wanted the music on my desk speakers and the HomePod in the next room at the same time. That's really all this does: it grabs the audio once and sends it everywhere you switch on.

Plays from

  • All system audio
  • One app, several apps, or every app except the ones you check
  • An input device, like a mic or an audio interface

Plays to, all at once

  • AirPlay speakers and Apple TVs
  • Google Cast speakers
  • Anything your computer can play to: built-in speakers, the headphone jack, HDMI, USB and Bluetooth

Why it echoes, and the fix

Every speaker takes its own time to make a sound. AirPlay holds audio for a moment before it plays, while a speaker plugged into the Mac plays right away. Same song, two start times: it sounds like it's playing twice.

AudioSync holds back every output attached to the Mac by its own amount, so they all play the same moment of the song at once. The AirPlay speakers stay matched to one another as well.

What's in it

A small Dock app with a main window, plus a compact panel in the menu bar for quick switching.

A delay for each speaker

Each local speaker sits anywhere from 2 seconds before to 20 seconds after the Mac's built-in speakers, and it changes live while you listen, so you can nudge it until the echo is gone.

Calibrate with the mic

Put the microphone where you listen. AudioSync plays a short sweep on the built-in speakers and then on a local speaker, and sets that speaker's delay from the difference. Calibrate All Speakers measures every speaker that's on, apart from headphones and virtual outputs, one after another, and nothing changes until you click Apply.

A remote on your phone

Turn on Web Remote, scan its QR code and enter the pairing code. Your phone can then switch speakers, set volume and delay, and show what's playing. It uses plain HTTP, so keep it to your home network.

Volume, your way

One master volume for everything, and a separate one on every speaker.

A 10-band equalizer

32 Hz to 16 kHz, ±12 dB, a preamp and 12 presets. It's applied once, so every speaker gets the same sound.

Now Playing

Artwork, title and artist, with play, pause and skip, when the music comes from Spotify or Music.

Zones and auto-connect

Put speakers that play together in a zone, let favorites connect on their own, and start AudioSync at login if you like.

Codes, just once

An Apple TV that asks for a code gets a prompt the first time. After that it connects without one. A speaker with a password asks for it once each time AudioSync starts.

Good to know first

How the timing and the speakers work.

  • There's a lag. AirPlay plays as late as the speaker asks for, plus 0.1 s that AudioSync adds. It goes up to 0.2 s only after a speaker had to skip a bit of audio, and comes back down by itself. You won't notice with music, but it gets in the way of video and games.
  • You set the delays. Every output except the computer's own speakers gets a delay you pick (AirPlay speakers share one), and AudioSync remembers it per device.
  • AirPlay 1 and AirPlay 2. AudioSync streams with its own AirPlay sender. Speakers that only do AirPlay 2 play as they are. A speaker that does both uses AirPlay 2, or the original AirPlay when it was paired that way or AirPlay 2 can't take it. Up to 16 AirPlay 2 speakers play at once, and a stereo pair or an Apple TV with the HomePods it plays through shows up as one speaker.
  • Google Cast plays one speaker at a time. Cast has no shared clock, so you set its offset by ear and AudioSync remembers it. A speaker group made in the Google Home app plays as one speaker.
  • It's a beta. Expect rough edges.

Coming from Airfoil?

The idea is the same: send the whole computer, or one app, to AirPlay speakers and local outputs together. AudioSync just does less. It plays to AirPlay receivers, Google Cast speakers (one at a time, with an offset you set by ear) and the outputs your computer already knows about, on macOS 15 or later and on Windows 11.

Get AudioSync

It's free. Grab the latest build, or build it yourself from the source.

Download for Mac

For macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. Open the disk image and drag AudioSync to Applications.

The disk image is about 5.9 MB, and the installed app takes about 18 MB. Besides its settings, AudioSync keeps a few small files: your AirPlay pairings, its AirPlay 2 identity, a timing cache per AirPlay 2 speaker, and the remote and Cast devices it knows. Your audio is never written to disk.

The builds aren't notarized yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. To get past it:

  1. If macOS says it can't verify AudioSync, click Done.
  2. Open Privacy & Security in System Settings.
  3. Scroll down, click Open Anyway and confirm with your password. You only do this once.
Latest release

Or, from Terminal. A copy fetched this way skips the extra step:

curl -fsSL https://github.com/yassersutojoyo/audiosync/releases/latest/download/install.sh | bash

Download for Windows

For Windows 11, on x64 or ARM64. Get the zip for your PC, unzip it into a folder of its own and run the exe. There's no installer, and from 0.8 it updates itself.

The exe isn't signed, so the first time you open it Windows SmartScreen may say it protected your PC. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Latest release

Or, from PowerShell. It checks the zip, adds a Start menu shortcut, needs no admin rights, and SmartScreen doesn't ask:

irm https://github.com/yassersutojoyo/audiosync/releases/latest/download/install.ps1 | iex

Download for Linux

For x86_64 or aarch64, with glibc 2.39 or newer and PipeWire. Get the AppImage for your machine, make it executable and run it. It updates itself. There's a .deb for amd64 and arm64 too.

Latest release

Or, from a terminal. It checks the AppImage and puts it in ~/.local/bin with a menu entry, no sudo:

curl -fsSL https://github.com/yassersutojoyo/audiosync/releases/latest/download/install-linux.sh | bash

Build from source

You'll need:

  • macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later
  • The Command Line Tools for Xcode 16 or later
  • A Rust toolchain (rustup)
git clone https://github.com/yassersutojoyo/audiosync
cd audiosync
./setup.sh

The first run needs a network connection and takes a while. After that, ./build.sh is all you need. The development notes have the details.
