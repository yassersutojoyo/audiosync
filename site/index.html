Every speaker in the house, on the same beat.

AudioSync plays your Mac's audio on AirPlay speakers and the Mac's own outputs all at once. It lines them up, so you hear one song instead of an echo. Think of it as a free Airfoil alternative that does less, on purpose.

One Mac, every speaker

I wanted the music on my desk speakers and the HomePod in the next room at the same time. That's really all this does: it grabs the audio once and sends it everywhere you switch on.

Plays from

  • Everything the Mac is playing
  • One app, along with its helper processes
  • An input, like a mic, a line-in or an audio interface

Plays to, all at once

  • AirPlay speakers and Apple TVs
  • Built-in speakers and the headphone jack
  • HDMI or DisplayPort, USB and Bluetooth outputs
  • A Google Cast speaker, or a Google Home group as one

Why it echoes, and the fix

Every speaker takes its own time to make a sound. AirPlay holds about two seconds of audio before it plays, while a speaker plugged into the Mac plays right away. Same song, two start times: it sounds like it's playing twice.

AudioSync holds back every output attached to the Mac by its own amount, so they all play the same moment of the song at once. The AirPlay speakers stay matched to one another as well.

What's in it

A small Dock app with a main window, plus a compact panel in the menu bar for quick switching.

A delay for each speaker

Each local speaker sits anywhere from 2 seconds before to 20 seconds after the Mac's built-in speakers, and it changes live while you listen, so you can nudge it until the echo is gone.

Calibrate all speakers

Put the microphone where you listen. AudioSync plays a short sweep on each speaker that's on, one at a time, and lists the delay it measured. Apply sets them all, Undo puts them all back.

A remote on your phone

Turn on Web Remote, scan its QR code and enter the pairing code. Your phone can then switch speakers, set volume and delay, and show what's playing. It uses plain HTTP, so keep it to your home network.

Volume, your way

One master volume for everything, and a separate one on every speaker.

A 10-band equalizer

32 Hz to 16 kHz, ±12 dB, a preamp and 12 presets. It's applied once, so every speaker gets the same sound.

Now Playing

Artwork, title and artist, with play, pause and skip, when the music comes from Spotify or Music.

Groups and auto-connect

Save a set of speakers as a group, let favorites connect on their own, and start AudioSync at login if you like.

Codes, just once

An Apple TV that asks for a code gets a prompt the first time. After that it connects without one. A speaker with a password asks for it once each time AudioSync starts.

Good to know first

A few limits to keep in mind before you install it.

  • There's a two-second lag. AirPlay adds a fixed delay of about 2 s, and nothing can shorten it. You won't notice with music, but it gets in the way of video and games.
  • You set the delays. Every output except the Mac's own speakers gets a delay you pick (AirPlay speakers share one), and AudioSync remembers it per device.
  • Not every speaker plays yet. AudioSync now streams with its own AirPlay sender, which speaks the original AirPlay for now, so a speaker that only takes AirPlay 2 won't play unless you turn on AirPlay 2 (Experimental) in the app menu or the gear menu, then quit and reopen AudioSync.
  • Pairs and HomePods are untested. With AirPlay 2 on, a stereo pair or a home theater group shows up as one speaker, but no real pair has been tried yet. I haven't tried the new sender on a HomePod yet, so if yours doesn't show up or play, please open an issue on GitHub with the debug log.
  • Track info is limited. Now Playing only picks up Spotify and Apple Music.
  • It's an early alpha. Expect rough edges.

Coming from Airfoil?

The idea is the same: send the whole Mac, or one app, to AirPlay speakers and local outputs together. AudioSync just does less. It plays to AirPlay receivers, Google Cast speakers and Google Home groups (one at a time, with an offset you set by ear) and the outputs macOS already knows about, and it needs macOS 15 or later. AirPlay 2 is still experimental and off by default.

Get AudioSync

It's free. Grab the latest build, or build it yourself from the source.

Download

For macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later. Open the disk image and drag AudioSync to Applications.

The builds aren't notarized yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open the app once, then click Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

Latest release

Build from source

You'll need:

  • macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later
  • The Command Line Tools for Xcode 16 or later
  • A Rust toolchain (rustup)
git clone https://github.com/yassersutojoyo/audiosync
cd audiosync
./setup.sh

The first run needs a network connection and takes a while. After that, ./build.sh is all you need. The development notes have the details.

Windows 11 and Linux versions are in the works too. They aren't out yet.
