Open Volume Mixer
The main window holds every control. The AudioSync icon in the notification area opens the AudioSync tray panel, with the Streaming switch, the source, the volume and the speakers. Closing the window keeps AudioSync running there, and streaming goes on. To quit, right-click the AudioSync icon in the notification area and choose Exit AudioSync, or press Ctrl+Q.\n\nUnder Audio Source, pick what to play: All System Audio, one app that plays sound, Several Apps, or an input device, such as a microphone or line in. Several Apps, at the end of the source menu, sends Only the Checked Apps or All Apps Except the Checked Ones. AudioSync keeps your last mix of apps, so you can switch back to it later. If Windows blocks microphone access for desktop apps, a notice says so, with a button that opens those privacy settings.\n\nThe speakers are listed under This PC (the PC's own speakers or line out, and headphones on its jack), then your zones, each with its speakers under it, then Connected Speakers: AirPlay and Google Cast speakers and every other output, such as USB, Bluetooth or HDMI, with a small icon after its name for its kind; point to the icon to read the kind.\n\nSelect a speaker's round button to turn it on; streaming starts with it. Select it again to turn it off. When you turn the Streaming switch off, your speakers stay chosen with an outlined button, and they play again when you turn streaming on or select any speaker.
A microphone can measure delays for you. Put it where you listen and keep the room quiet. Each measurement compares a speaker with the PC's own speakers, so they have to be on and playing, and streaming has to be on too.\n\nTo measure one local speaker, turn it on, select its row or its delay, and select Calibrate with Microphone under the delay controls. Headphones and virtual outputs don't have that button. AudioSync fades the music out, plays a short, quiet sweep on the PC's own speakers and then on this speaker, and sets its delay from the difference. Cancel stops it and leaves the delay as it was.\n\nCalibrate All Speakers, the row under Equalizer, measures every speaker that is on, AirPlay and Google Cast included. Headphones, headsets, earbuds and virtual outputs are left out, since the microphone can't hear them, and their delay stays yours to set; the list under the row names them with the reason. If a speaker reports itself as headphones or a virtual output, Include Anyway measures it with the others until you close the results. AudioSync fades the music out, plays a sweep on each speaker in turn, twice each, and fades the music back in. A Google Cast speaker with no offset yet can take up to a minute. The results show each speaker's old and new delay; the AirPlay speakers share one AirPlay delay, the middle of their values. Nothing changes until you choose Apply. Undo puts the old delays back, and Measure Again repeats one speaker. If the speakers, the source or a delay change during a run, it stops and no delay changes.\n\nTo calibrate one zone, right-click it or use its … button and choose Calibrate Zone. It measures the zone's speakers that are on, as Calibrate All Speakers does; speakers outside the zone stay quiet during the run and keep their delays.\n\nA row that wasn't measured clearly says why, and its delay stays. The microphone may have barely heard the speaker, been overloaded, or heard an echo almost as loud as the sweep, or the two sweeps differed too much. Turn the speaker up or down, move the microphone or quiet the room, then select Measure Again on that row. One speaker's calibration says the same under its button and leaves the delay as it was; select Calibrate with Microphone again to retry.\n\nIf Windows blocks microphone access for desktop apps, AudioSync says so and where to allow it.
Each speaker has its own volume slider, and Master Volume sets the level of all of them at once, AirPlay and Google Cast speakers included. Local speakers' sliders follow how loudness is heard: halfway is about −18 dB. AirPlay speakers use the AirPlay volume scale. A Google Cast speaker's slider sets the speaker's own volume, and Master Volume scales it like every other speaker. Mute under it mutes the speaker itself.\n\nWhen a local speaker that is on is turned down low or muted in Windows, a line under its slider says so, with a button that fixes it. AudioSync changes the Windows volume only when you select that button.\n\nAirPlay adds at most 0.4 s on top of what the speaker needs. The first 0.1 s is there from the start, and the rest comes only when packets are lost. Once it comes, it stays for that speaker, and the speaker starts there again when it rejoins, until AudioSync quits.
Windows keeps playing the source on its own output while AudioSync plays its synced copies. So you don't hear it twice, AudioSync moves the source to a virtual output nobody listens to while it streams, and moves it back when streaming stops. For All System Audio, that output becomes the Windows default; for one app, only that app is moved.\n\nWhen it can't, a speaker the source already plays on gets no copy from AudioSync. Volume, EQ and delay don't apply to it.\n\nOn a PC with no virtual output, AudioSync offers to add VB-CABLE by VB-Audio as one. To remove it, uninstall VB-CABLE under Settings > Apps > Installed apps, or run VB-Audio's setup again and choose Remove Driver, then restart Windows. AudioSync then offers it again when the source plays out loud.\n\nDon't mute the app itself in the Volume mixer: AudioSync would capture silence.
To make a zone, turn on the speakers you want, then choose New Zone from Active Speakers in the gear menu (Ctrl+N). They show under the zone, and a speaker is in one zone at most. Each one also keeps a short row under This PC or Connected Speakers: its round button there turns only that speaker on or off, and the link after its name goes to its zone. Select the zone's round icon to turn its speakers on or off at once; select anywhere else on its header to show or hide them. Each zone has its own volume. To play one speaker of a zone alone, choose Only This Speaker in its menu under the zone; the others turn off and stay in the zone. For a mix of speakers you turn on again and again, save a preset instead. To rename a zone, double-click its name; to rename or delete it, right-click it or use its … button. F2 renames the zone you're on. Deleting a zone gives its speakers their full rows back under This PC or Connected Speakers. To move a speaker, right-click it or use its … button and choose Move to Zone or Remove from Zone.\n\nTo save a preset, turn on the speakers you want and set their volumes, the zone volumes, the master volume and the curve, then choose Save as Preset… from the Presets row. Delays and the preamp aren't saved. To recall a preset, choose it from the Presets row, pick it in the tray panel that opens from the AudioSync icon in the notification area, or press Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 for the first nine. Speakers you hid are never saved in a preset, and recalling one leaves them hidden. To rename, update or delete a preset, open the Presets row and use the … button on the preset's row, or right-click it.\n\nTo rename a speaker, double-click its name, or right-click it or use its … button and choose Rename. Type a name and press Enter. Leave it empty to use the device's own name; while a speaker is renamed, its tooltip also shows the device's own name.\n\nTo have a speaker turn on whenever it appears, right-click it or use its … button, and choose Auto-Connect When Available. At launch, AudioSync starts streaming only when such a speaker is there.\n\nTo keep a speaker you never use out of the lists, choose Hide Speaker in its menu. A speaker that is on turns off first. Hidden Speakers in Settings lists what you hid; select Unhide beside one to show it again, off, with its volume and delay as you left them.\n\nThe equalizer shapes the sound on every speaker at once, with ten bands, a preamp, preset curves and curves you save yourself. Open it from its row or with Ctrl+E.\n\nWhile an app has a track in the media controls Windows shows, the Now Playing card in the main window shows it with its artwork, Previous Track, Play or Pause, and Next Track. When the app offers them, Shuffle and Repeat show too. With All System Audio or Several Apps, that's the track that is playing, else the paused track of the app that played last; with one app, that app's track. With an input device, the card is hidden.\n\nAirPlay speakers with a screen, such as a TV, show the track's title, artist, album and artwork, taken from the media controls Windows shows for apps that support them. With All System Audio, that's the track that is playing; with one app, that app's track, even while paused. Otherwise they show AudioSync, the source's name, the album From your PC and AudioSync's icon.\n\nVirtual outputs, such as loopback drivers, are hidden unless one is on. Show Virtual Devices in Settings lists them all. Refresh Devices in the gear menu (F5) reads the sources and outputs again.\n\nTo start AudioSync when you sign in to Windows, turn on Launch at Sign-In in Settings, or Launch at Sign-In in the menu of the AudioSync icon in the notification area. AudioSync then starts there without opening its window. If AudioSync is turned off in Settings > Apps > Startup, that page opens with a notice: turn AudioSync on there.\n\nAppearance in Settings: Match System follows Windows; Light or Dark keeps AudioSync's windows that way. The AudioSync icon in the notification area always follows the Windows theme.\n\nAudioSync saves the source, each speaker's switch, delay, volume and name, the speakers you hid, your zones and the equalizer, and restores them the next time it starts.
AudioSync finds AirPlay speakers on your network by itself. If none show up, check that this PC is on the same network as your speakers and that Windows Firewall allows AudioSync on that network. If Windows calls the network Public, allow AudioSync on Public networks, or set the network to Private. For a HomePod, check its access in the Home app on an iPhone, iPad or Mac: Home Settings → Speakers & TV → Allow Speaker & TV Access → “Anyone on the Same Network”.\n\nAudioSync for Windows plays to AirPlay 1 and AirPlay 2 receivers. A speaker that needs something AudioSync for Windows can't do yet isn't listed.\n\nAn Apple TV, or a speaker reached over AirPlay 2, that asks for a code shows “Asks for a code”. When you turn it on, it shows a code: type it in the dialog that opens. AudioSync keeps the pairing, so the code isn't asked again. Forget Pairing in the speaker's … menu removes it. Auto-connect never starts a pairing: it shows a notice instead. A speaker set to ask for a code every time shows “Asks for a code every time”. Auto-connect leaves it off, and AudioSync asks for the code each time you turn it on.\n\nA speaker protected by a password shows “Asks for a code” too. When you turn it on, type the password set for it in the Home app or in the speaker's settings. AudioSync keeps it in memory until it quits, so it asks once each time AudioSync starts. Forget Password in the speaker's … menu drops it and turns the speaker off, so AudioSync asks for it again the next time you turn the speaker on. A wrong password brings the dialog back; Cancel leaves the speaker off. If a speaker turns the password down after it has started playing, it goes off with a notice. A speaker that auto-connect turned on goes off with a notice instead: turn it on yourself to type the password.\n\nIf AudioSync's AirPlay sender stops or can't start, a notice says so, with a Retry button.\n\nA speaker that plays both AirPlay 1 and AirPlay 2 uses AirPlay 2, or AirPlay 1 when it was paired over AirPlay 1 or AirPlay 2 can't take it; then its row says AirPlay. A stereo pair or home theater group shows as one row, and its note names the speakers in it.
Control AudioSync from a phone on the same network as this PC: each speaker's switch, volume and delay, Master Volume, the source or a mix of several apps, each zone's switch and volume, and the presets. It also shows what's playing. Choose Web Remote… in the gear menu, turn Web Remote on, and open the address it shows on the phone (or scan its QR code). Add the page to the Home Screen before you enter the code, then open AudioSync from the new icon and pair there: on iPhone, a pairing made in Safari doesn't carry over to the Home Screen app. The window shows these steps for iPhone and Android. Each code works once and for 10 minutes; the window shows the next one, and New Code makes a fresh one.\n\nThe page uses plain HTTP, so use the web remote only on your home network. On a network Windows calls Public, the window and a notice say so. Forget All Remote Devices unpairs every phone, also while the remote is off. If another app already uses the port, the window says so and lets you pick another one.
