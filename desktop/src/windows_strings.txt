The main window holds every control. The AudioSync icon in the notification area opens AudioSync quick settings, with the Streaming switch, the source, the volume and the speakers. Closing the window keeps AudioSync running there, and streaming goes on. To quit, choose File > Exit.\n\nUnder Audio Source, pick what to play: All System Audio, one app that plays sound, or an input device, such as a microphone or line in. If Windows blocks microphone access for desktop apps, a notice says so, with a button that opens those privacy settings.\n\nThe speakers are listed under This PC (the PC's own speakers or line out, and headphones on its jack), then your zones, each with its speakers under it, then Connected Speakers: AirPlay and Google Cast speakers and every other output, such as USB, Bluetooth or HDMI, with its kind under its name.\n\nSelect a speaker's round button to turn it on; streaming starts with it. Select it again to turn it off. When you turn the Streaming switch off, your speakers stay chosen with an outlined button, and they play again when you turn streaming on or select any speaker.
A microphone can measure delays for you. Put it where you listen and keep the room quiet. Each measurement compares a speaker with the PC's own speakers, so they have to be on and playing, and streaming has to be on too.\n\nTo measure one local speaker, turn it on, select its row or its delay, and select Calibrate with Microphone under the delay controls. Headphones and virtual outputs don't have that button. AudioSync fades the music out, plays a short, quiet sweep on the PC's own speakers and then on this speaker, and sets its delay from the difference. Cancel stops it and leaves the delay as it was.\n\nCalibrate All Speakers, on the Calibration card under Equalizer, measures every speaker that is on, AirPlay and Google Cast included. Headphones, headsets, earbuds and virtual outputs are left out, since the microphone can't hear them, and their delay stays yours to set; the card lists them with the reason. If a speaker reports itself as headphones or a virtual output, Include Anyway measures it with the others until you close the results. AudioSync fades the music out, plays a sweep on each speaker in turn, twice each, and fades the music back in. The results show each speaker's old and new delay; the AirPlay speakers share one AirPlay delay, the middle of their values. Nothing changes until you choose Apply. Undo puts the old delays back, and Measure Again repeats one speaker.\n\nIf Windows blocks microphone access for desktop apps, AudioSync says so and where to allow it.
Each speaker has its own volume slider, and Master Volume sets the level of all of them at once, AirPlay and Google Cast speakers included. A Google Cast speaker's slider sets the speaker's own volume, and Master Volume scales it like every other speaker. Mute under it mutes the speaker itself.\n\nWhen a local speaker that is on is turned down low or muted in Windows, a line under its slider says so, with a button that fixes it. AudioSync changes the Windows volume only when you select that button.
Windows keeps playing the source on its own outputs while AudioSync plays its synced copies, so you may hear it twice. For one app, open the Volume mixer and send the app to an output you don't listen to. For All System Audio, make such an output the Windows default in Sound settings. Don't mute the app itself in the Volume mixer: AudioSync would capture silence.\n\nWhen AudioSync detects this, it shows a notice. Its Show How button opens the steps for your setup.
To make a zone, turn on the speakers you want, then choose Speakers > New Zone from Active Speakers (Ctrl+N). They show under the zone, and a speaker is in one zone at most. Each one also keeps a short row under This PC or Connected Speakers: select its round button there to play it by itself, or the link after its name to go to its zone. Select the zone's name to turn its speakers on or off at once; its arrow shows or hides them. Each zone has its own volume. To play one speaker of a zone alone, choose Only This Speaker in its menu under the zone; the others turn off and stay in the zone. For a mix of speakers you turn on again and again, save a preset instead. To rename or delete a zone, right-click it or use its … button; F2 renames the zone you're on. Deleting a zone gives its speakers their full rows back under This PC or Connected Speakers. To move a speaker, right-click it or use its … button and choose Move to Zone or Remove from Zone.\n\nTo save a preset, turn on the speakers you want and set their volumes, the zone volumes, the master volume and the curve, then select Save as Preset… in the Presets card or in View > Presets. Delays and the preamp aren't saved. To recall a preset, select it in the card, pick it in the flyout that opens from the AudioSync icon in the notification area, or press Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 for the first nine. Speakers you hid are never saved in a preset, and recalling one leaves them hidden. To rename, update or delete a preset, right-click it or use its … button.\n\nTo rename a speaker, double-click its name, or right-click it or use its … button and choose Rename. Type a name and press Enter. Leave it empty to use the device's own name; while a speaker is renamed, its tooltip shows that name.\n\nTo have a speaker turn on whenever it appears, right-click it or use its … button, and choose Auto-Connect When Available. At launch, AudioSync starts streaming only when such a speaker is there.\n\nTo keep a speaker you never use out of the lists, choose Hide Speaker in its menu. A speaker that is on turns off first. Hidden Speakers in the Speakers menu lists what you hid; choose one to show it again, off, with its volume and delay as you left them.\n\nThe equalizer shapes the sound on every speaker at once, with ten bands, a preamp, preset curves and curves you save yourself. Open it from its card or with View > Equalizer (Ctrl+E).\n\nAirPlay speakers with a screen, such as a TV, show the track's title, artist, album and artwork, taken from the media controls Windows shows for apps that support them. With All System Audio, that's the track that is playing; with one app, that app's track, even while paused. Otherwise they show AudioSync, the source's name, the album From your PC and AudioSync's icon.\n\nVirtual outputs, such as loopback drivers, are hidden unless one is on. View > Show Virtual Devices lists them all. Speakers > Refresh Devices (F5) reads the sources and outputs again.\n\nTo start AudioSync when you sign in to Windows, choose File > Launch at Sign-In, or Launch at Sign-In in the menu of the AudioSync icon in the notification area. AudioSync then starts there without opening its window. If AudioSync is turned off in Settings > Apps > Startup, that page opens with a notice: turn AudioSync on there.\n\nView > Appearance: Match System follows Windows; Light or Dark keeps AudioSync's windows that way. The AudioSync icon in the notification area always follows the Windows theme.\n\nAudioSync saves the source, each speaker's switch, delay, volume and name, the speakers you hid, your zones and the equalizer, and restores them the next time it starts.

If it has to stay Public, allow AudioSync on Public networks in Windows Firewall.\n\nAudioSync for Windows plays to AirPlay 1 receivers. A speaker that plays only AirPlay 2, or needs something else AudioSync for Windows can't do yet, is listed with a note saying so, and stays off unless AirPlay 2 (Experimental) is on.\n\nAn Apple TV that asks for a PIN shows “Asks for a code”. When you turn it on, the TV shows a PIN: type it in the dialog that opens. AudioSync keeps the pairing, so the PIN isn't asked again. Forget Pairing in the speaker's … menu removes it. Auto-connect never starts a pairing: it shows a notice instead. A speaker set to ask for a code every time shows “Asks for a code every time”. Auto-connect leaves it off, and AudioSync asks for the code each time you turn it on.\n\nA speaker protected by a password shows “Asks for a code” too. When you turn it on, type the password set for it in the Home app or in the speaker's settings. AudioSync keeps it in memory until it quits, so it asks once each time AudioSync starts. A wrong password brings the dialog back; Cancel leaves the speaker off. If a speaker turns the password down after it has started playing, it goes off with a notice. A speaker that auto-connect turned on goes off with a notice instead: turn it on yourself to type the password.\n\nIf AudioSync's AirPlay sender stops or can't start, a notice says so, with a Retry button.\n\nAirPlay 2 (Experimental), in the File menu, lets AudioSync play to AirPlay 2 speakers, stereo pairs and home theater groups. It's off at first; turn it on, then quit and reopen AudioSync. With it on, a stereo pair or home theater group shows as one row, and its note names the speakers in it.
A phone on the same network as this PC can switch your speakers on and off, and set their volume, their delay and the master volume. It also shows what's playing. Choose File > Web Remote…, turn Web Remote on, and open the address it shows on the phone (or scan its QR code). Add the page to the Home Screen before you enter the code, then open AudioSync from the new icon and pair there: on iPhone, a pairing made in Safari doesn't carry over to the Home Screen app. The window shows these steps for iPhone and Android. Each code works once and for 10 minutes; the window shows the next one, and New Code makes a fresh one.\n\nThe page uses plain HTTP, so use the web remote only on your home network. On a network Windows calls Public, the window and a notice say so. Forget All Remote Devices unpairs every phone, also while the remote is off. If another app already uses the port, the window says so and lets you pick another one.
